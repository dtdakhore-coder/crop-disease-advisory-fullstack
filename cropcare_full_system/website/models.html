Practical choices for an academic prototype. Where we start: transfer learning with MobileNetV2 or EfficientNet, plus rule-based advice.

Why Transfer Learning?

Training a heavy model from zero needs huge datasets and compute. Transfer learning keeps training and deployment manageable.

MobileNetV2

Inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks (Sandler et al., CVPR 2018). Compact, fast, ideal for mobile deployment where farmers actually use it.

EfficientNet

Rethinking model scaling for CNNs (Tan & Le, ICML 2019). Compound scaling gives excellent accuracy-to-size ratio.

Rule-Based Advisory

A lookup database linking crop + disease to advice. No second ML model needed at first, and experts can update advice without retraining.

Limits, Validation & Future Scope

Being honest about what it can and cannot do.

Keep in Mind

Results depend on photo quality and training data. Dim light, blurry shots and the gap between lab images and messy field conditions can lower accuracy. Similar diseases can look alike. Advice is general guidance, not a prescription. Before anyone relies on it in practice, the model should be tried on photos of local crops and agricultural experts should review the advice it gives.

Where We Can Take It

More crops and diseases · soil and nutrient analysis · weather data · voice support in Marathi and Hindi · bigger, more varied field datasets · offline use in low-connectivity areas.
